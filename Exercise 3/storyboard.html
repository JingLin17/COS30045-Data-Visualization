The Data Story: What Really Drives Your TV's Power Bill?

Thinking about a new TV? Two things quietly decide how much it costs to run each year: how big it is, and what kind of screen it uses.

Size is the biggest factor

Grouping TVs into small (under 43"), medium (44"–65") and large (over 66") shows a clear pattern: average energy consumption drops from about 745 kWh/year for large TVs, to 405 kWh/year for medium, to just 155 kWh/year for small. If keeping running costs down matters to you, size is the first thing to check.

But screen technology matters too

Comparing LCD, LCD LED and OLED panels within each size category reveals a second effect: OLED and LCD LED screens use more energy than plain LCD at every size. The surprise is at the small end — small OLED TVs use nearly double the energy of a similarly-sized LCD or LCD LED (about 232 kWh/year vs around 120 kWh/year), despite being the smaller option.

Takeaway: if running cost is a priority, start with a smaller screen size — then double-check the panel technology, since a compact OLED can quietly cost more to run than a larger LCD.
